## Share text

When your AI assistant acts "on behalf of" you, does it get a slice of your access, or a full copy?

For most agents today, it's a copy. The agent runs on the person's own token, holds everything they can reach, and every log line says the person did it.

"Book my trip and expense it" sounds like one request. It's really four kinds of access: read the calendar, read email, book with an outside provider, and move money. Each one deserves a different answer.

So this week I turned that into a five-question decision tree: is a person asking, does the resource trust your authorization server, is enterprise-managed authorization available, is the action high risk, and is another agent involved.

Inside the article: a scenario you'll probably recognize, the agent-to-agent rules that stop quiet privilege escalation, and one audit question to ask about every agent this week.

Agentic Identity Notes, week 8. When an agent acts for one of your people, does the token say so, or does it just say it's them?
